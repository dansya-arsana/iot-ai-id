# Foreign executive critique: the AIoT interactive Indonesia map hero

**Persona:** VP Global Business Development at a Japanese/Korean maker of AMRs, cobots and inspection robots. I am choosing between Thailand, Vietnam, Malaysia and Indonesia for our next market. Two other executives sit at the table:
- **(B)** CEO of a German company making inspection drones and sensors.
- **(C)** Founder of a US company making field robots for agriculture and mining.

---

## 1. The 10-second test

**Verdict: the map catches my eye but does not tell me why I should come.** A lit-up archipelago tells me Indonesia is big. I already know that. A map of geography does not answer my real question: can I sell, certify and service AMRs here, and what proof do I get?

**What would hook me (needs to be visible in the first 10 seconds, before I hover):**
- One line that names the problem I have: *"Your robot, proven at 35 °C and 80%+ humidity, on unstable power, in real Indonesian factories, with a path to Komdigi certification, a local service partner and a test report you can show your board."*
- Real sites where AIoT actually operates, shown as pins on the map. I need to see the difference between "places Indonesia has" and "places you can put my robot next month".
- Java lit up by default for my industry. 106 of the 179 industrial estates and about 57% of GDP are there. That answers "where do I start" without making me explore.

**What would bore me:**
- Island land areas, Lake Toba, the Weber Deep, the vanishing glacier, peat hectares, island counts. These are interesting for tourists. For me they are noise.
- Eight regions with equal weight. For an AMR or cobot company, Maluku and Papua are not equal to Java. Treating them as equal shows that the site has not segmented its buyers.

> **(B)** "I disagree a little. Haze, peat fires and salt-fog are exactly what I want for drones and sensors. The map works for me, *if* it leads with the stressor and not the hectares."
> **(C)** "For me Kalimantan coal and Merauke farmland are the story. But this is a homepage. You can't make all three of us explore eight regions. Let me pick my industry and re-colour the map."

## 3. What is missing, and what I would need to see

1. **What AIoT actually owns or has access to.** Named host sites, the type of each facility, and any lab equipment: climate chamber, IP and dust rigs, salt-fog chamber. Is the lab accredited by KAN or under ISO/IEC 17025? Or is it field observation only?
2. **Certification path, step by step, with weeks and rough cost:**
   - Komdigi type approval (Permenkominfo 3/2024), with a clear status for the 4G/5G TKDN rule.
   - SNI checked against each HS code.
   - Importer of record (API-U or API-P).
   - Whether a PT PMA is needed, or whether you act as distributor of record.
3. **After-sales.** A map of service coverage, local stock of spare parts, RMA turnaround time, and technicians trained by whom.
4. **Pilot economics.** A price band, how long it takes, what is delivered (a sample report), and how success is measured.
5. **IP and data.** Who owns the datasets. An NDA template. Camera and floor data on factory sites under the PDP Law, and the rules for sending that data abroad. Whether data stays in Indonesia. Firmware and IP protection while the robot is in the field.
6. **Liability and insurance** while my robot sits on a third party's site.
7. **Proof.** Case studies, logos of client or host sites, named team members with LinkedIn profiles, and company registration details (NIB, address).
8. **A comparison with Thailand, Malaysia and Vietnam.** That is the decision I am actually making. One honest comparison table beats eight regions.
9. Support in Japanese and Korean, or at least a statement that you work with our time zones and our documentation formats.

## 4. Map filters I would actually use

- **Industry:** manufacturing and electronics, warehousing and logistics, mining, oil and gas and energy, ports and maritime, agriculture and plantations, healthcare and hospitality. This is the main filter, and it should re-colour the map.
- **Stressor:** heat and humidity, dust and haze, unstable power, weak connectivity, salt and corrosion, seismic activity and vibration.
- **Readiness:** hours from Jakarta or Singapore, service coverage, and where AIoT has active sites.
- **Incentive zones:** KEK and FTZ overlays (Batam, Kendal, Gresik, Sanur and others).
- A **toggle for data confidence and sources**, off by default and available to sceptics like me.

> **(C)** "Without the Industry filter I'd bounce. With it, the map is the best part of the site."

"Test here" is too vague. I don't know what happens next. Is it a sales call, a quote or a form that goes nowhere?

**Primary CTA:** **"Get a pilot plan for your robot: site, certification path, timeline and cost, within 5 working days."**

**Secondary CTA:** **"Download the Indonesia market-entry brief for robotics (PDF, sources included)."** This is the one I would forward to my CEO.

**On each region card:** *"Scope a [factory / port / mine] pilot in [Java]"*. The card should already name the scenario.

## 6. Red flags that would make me leave

- Numbers with no source, or the 2026 hotspot counts shown as if they were normal statistics.
- An inaccurate map: wrong shapes, no context for Timor-Leste and Papua New Guinea, or Batam missing.
- **Scroll-jacking.** A full-screen hero is fine. Hijacking my scroll wheel or trapping my trackpad is not. Make the first scroll go to section 2 cleanly, let the browser's back and forward still work, and make keyboard users able to get past the hero.
- Content that only appears on hover, which disappears on touch devices. A tablet in a meeting room is my real device.
- A heavy map that delays page load by 3 seconds or more on hotel Wi-Fi.
- Claiming "all of Indonesia" when the company plainly operates in one or two places.
- Promotional framing of contested sites (Morowali, Weda Bay, Merauke, Flores geothermal), or disaster death tolls used as a hook.
- "Invitation-only" or gated areas with no explanation of who you are.
- Using the word "certified" without saying who certified it.

---

## 7. Score: **5/10** as described

**The idea is strong. As described, it is a geography lesson, not a sales tool.**

**What would make it a 9:**
1. An **Industry filter** at the top that re-colours the map. Java and Batam are lit up by default.
2. **Real AIoT site pins**, shown separately from "regions Indonesia has".
3. Each region card holds **one stressor, one named test scenario, one sourced number with a confidence badge, and one CTA**. Everything else is deleted.
4. Under the hero, a strip showing **Certify, Import, Pilot, Distribute and Service**, with a number of weeks for each step.
5. **One real case study**, even an anonymised one, plus a sample test-report PDF.
6. The map is fast and works on touch. The first scroll goes cleanly to section 2, with no scroll-jacking.

> **(B)** "I'd give it 6. It's better for sensor and drone companies like mine."
> **(C)** "I'd give it 5. Same fix: filter by industry first, show geography second."

# Field test: Dutch agri-robotics CTO reviews the Indonesia map hero

*Persona: CTO of a Dutch company making autonomous sprayers, harvest-assist robots and drones, deployed in Europe and Brazil. Two others are in the debate: **Kenji**, an industrial AMR/cobot VP from Japan, and **Ingrid**, a Norwegian aquaculture and AUV founder.*

---

## 1. The 10-second test

**Verdict: not yet.** A glowing map says "big, diverse country". That is true and every investment-promotion site already says it. My robots don't care about islands. They care about crops, soil, season and labour.

**What would hook me**, if it appeared as a headline over the map before any hover:
- "7 provinces produce 81.67% of Indonesia's palm oil (Kementan 2024). Harvest is still manual. Test where the labour gap is."
- Palm at 16.83 M ha (2023), and harvester shortages reported by GAPKI.
- Agriculture employs 40.76 M people (28% of workers), and Kementan itself says farmers are ageing.

**What bores me:** island counts (17,380 vs 17,504), the coastline argument, Lake Toba, Kerinci, the glacier, sea area against land area. That is geography trivia.

> **Kenji:** "Same for me. I need Java and Batam factories. Climate trivia is noise. Default to the industrial view and I'm happy."
